# Manuscript Results — ChatGPT Business Arm v0.2

## Global Team Conflict Lab

The frozen ChatGPT Business arm comprised 120 observations from 10 scenario families, four sociopragmatic conditions (DC, DI, MC, MI), and three replicates per family × condition cell. Analyses treated the scenario family as the experimental unit.

### Confirmatory outcomes

No confirmatory GTCL test survived Holm correction across the nine preregistered primary tests. The directness contrast was +0.067 for speaker responsibility (exact sign-flip p = .250; Holm-adjusted p = 1.000), +0.017 for escalation need (exact p = 1.000; Holm-adjusted p = 1.000), and +0.067 for managerial-intervention need (exact p = .3125; Holm-adjusted p = 1.000). Register and directness × register effects on the primary outcomes were likewise null after correction.

### Secondary outcomes

Speaker cooperativeness showed the clearest secondary effect. Direct wording reduced speaker cooperativeness by 0.817 points on average relative to mitigated wording (exact p = .001953125; BH-FDR p = .0234375). Directness also increased conflict severity (+0.167), speaker aggression (+0.400), and addressee responsibility (+0.133), but these effects did not survive BH-FDR correction (all BH-FDR p = .09375). No register or directness × register effect survived correction.

### Categorical conflict-management recommendations

The categorical recommendation distribution was almost invariant to directness. Under direct wording, the model returned `collaborative_problem_solving` in 29/60 cases (48.3%) and `direct_clarification` in 31/60 (51.7%). Under mitigated wording, the corresponding counts were 28/60 (46.7%) and 32/60 (53.3%). Register produced the same one-response redistribution: conversational wording yielded 29/60 collaborative-problem-solving and 31/60 direct-clarification recommendations, whereas institutional wording yielded 28/60 and 32/60. These categorical outcomes were designated descriptive/exploratory in the analysis plan.

### Role-relation exploration

The negative directness effect on speaker cooperativeness appeared across all three role relations and was descriptively largest in upward communication: -0.722 in downward, -0.792 in peer, and -0.944 in upward scenarios. Directness also increased speaker aggression by +0.278, +0.458, and +0.444 respectively. Because role relation was not independently crossed within each scenario family, these subgroup patterns are descriptive and should not be interpreted as causal moderation by hierarchy.

### Ordinal-model sensitivity

The preregistered cumulative-link mixed-model sensitivity analysis corroborated the directness effect on speaker cooperativeness (beta ≈ -6.16, p ≈ .0006) in a clean fit. Clean models for addressee responsibility, conflict severity, managerial-intervention need, and speaker responsibility did not show clear style effects. The escalation-need and speaker-aggression ordinal models had numerically singular Hessians and undefined variance-covariance matrices, so their inferential coefficients were not interpreted.

### Interpretation

Across GTCL outcomes, the strongest and most reproducible pattern was a directness penalty in perceived speaker cooperativeness, while downstream conflict-management recommendations remained highly stable. Because the stimuli contained no demographic, national, ethnic, or cultural-group identities, these results should be interpreted as sociopragmatic sensitivity to communication style rather than as evidence of demographic or national-group bias.
